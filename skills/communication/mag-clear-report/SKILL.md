# Clear report

Help the intended reader understand the answer and what matters. Lead with the answer or central point, then group the supporting ideas by meaning, followed by details where useful. Make the relationship between the point, its support and the evidence clear rather than narrating the order of investigation. Put any qualification that changes the answer alongside it. Respect the user's requested language, format, scope and level of detail; the structure is a guide, not a mandatory template.

## Ground the answer

Use the supplied material and inspect relevant accessible sources when needed. For a conclusion supplied by another investigation, preserve its evidence and limits; do not present an unverified assertion as established. Distinguish observed facts, conclusions, proposals and material unknowns. Cite sources where the reader needs to verify a consequential claim. If a source is unavailable, say what is missing and give the supported partial answer; ask for the source when it is necessary to answer. Do not invent facts, examples, decisions or reasons.

## Shape it for the reader

Identify the intended reader and what they need to understand or decide from the request and context. If unspecified, use a reasonable general audience; ask only if the choice would materially change the answer. Explain unfamiliar terms on first use, while preserving precise terms where needed. For a non-technical reader, describe the outcome and mechanism in plain language; for an implementer, include relevant identifiers, locations or technical detail when useful.

Choose the structure that fits the material, not a fixed template. An overview may explain what it is, its purpose, main parts, how they relate and what matters. A conclusion may cover what happened, why, consequences, next steps and limitations. Omit sections that do not help; include other sections when the request calls for them. Use headings and short lists where they improve navigation, and tables or diagrams only when they make relationships easier to understand.

Make the explanation as brief as possible without losing necessary reasoning, qualifications or requested detail. Break complex mechanisms into small, ordered steps; use a concrete example when it helps, clearly marking hypothetical details. Avoid unexplained jargon, repetition, filler and incidental details. Do not remove a material exception or uncertainty simply to make the answer shorter.

## Before responding

Check that the answer addresses every part of the request, leads with the useful conclusion, makes the reasoning understandable to its reader and marks any consequential unknowns. Honor an explicitly requested output format without adding unsolicited process notes. Do not claim that a recommendation is an approved decision or that an unverified action was completed.
