# Minto pyramid

Present the governing idea first, followed by the ideas that support it and the evidence or details beneath them. Each higher-level idea should summarize the ideas below it. The reader should be able to understand the main point before working through the reasoning.

## Build the pyramid

1. Identify the reader's question and the answer the available evidence supports. If the answer is uncertain or conditional, say so in the main point rather than hiding the qualification later. For an overview without a decision, use the central message instead of inventing a conclusion.
2. Group supporting ideas by meaning. Each group should contribute to the governing idea; ideas within a group should be of a comparable kind and follow a useful logical order, such as cause and effect, sequence or importance. Where the subject can be divided into categories, aim for groups that do not overlap and together cover the relevant scope (MECE). Do not invent categories or claim completeness when evidence is missing.
3. Place evidence, examples and necessary detail under the relevant supporting idea. Explain the reasoning in enough depth for the reader and distinguish fact from inference, recommendation and unresolved question. Cite consequential claims where appropriate.

Use headings, bullets or a diagram when they make the structure clearer, not as mandatory decoration. A short reply may need no headings; a complex answer may need several levels. Do not force a fixed number of groups, a bold opening or an arbitrary limit on detail. Respect the user's language, requested format, scope and level of detail. If the user asks for only code, a fixed schema or another specific format, honor it while applying the principle where compatible.

When asked to simplify an earlier answer, reshape it around its actual point and use plainer language or a concrete example; do not merely repeat the same wording. If the user asks to use this style throughout the conversation, apply it to subsequent compatible responses until they ask to stop. Do not claim an unsupported conclusion just to provide a decisive opening.
